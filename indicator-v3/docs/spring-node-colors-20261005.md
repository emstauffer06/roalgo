# Signed displacement node colors

The unsealed SpringNodeColors presentation module colors all 456 moving nodes: 384 across the eight layer array, 48 in the large companion, and 24 in the small display. It also colors the companion's 48 enlarged sphere adornments. Anchors, rest ticks, beams, and the preserved native research rig retain their colors.

Appearance revision: the user found the original colors too pale under Neon bloom. The palette now uses dark neutral RGB (52,58,66), saturated red (200,12,24), and saturated green (8,178,38). Tint intensity follows the square root of absolute normalized lean, making weak and moderate displacements visibly redder or greener while preserving the zero and full-tint endpoints. Material and global lighting are unchanged. Installed in place and compiled; all 456 nodes and 48 adornments updated, with unchanged paused row/camera and zero UI errors. The earlier RGB accuracy and timing evidence below describes the initial linear palette.

Green means positive X displacement from the node's resting position; red means negative displacement. Neutral gray marks zero. Intensity grows with absolute displacement, reaching full tint at one native displacement unit (64 native studs; 16 displayed studs in the small model). The scale is fixed across nodes and does not indicate profitability, a BUY/SELL decision, or probability. The 511-entry palette is quantized to 8-bit RGB and compared with engine-precision tolerance to avoid redundant writes.

Color is derived from the currently eased position and a cached rest-part reference, so it follows visible motion and remains valid when a whole model is translated. The small display's endpoint X attribute is deliberately not used. Parked models are skipped. Only Color and Color3 properties are written; cleanup restores their original values.

Future SpringArrayReplay controllers mount the module with autoUpdate=false and update it immediately after motion transitions, advances, and pause rendering. The active session received the module in place with reversible wrappers on the cached display Motion class, avoiding an archive reload or playback restart. CoreGui.RoAlgoSpringNodeColorControl exposes status/update/exit for that current-session adapter. Its wrappers and colors are restored when the array controller closes.

Both edited sources compile. Studio acceptance checked all 456 nodes and 48 adornments: maximum normalized RGB error versus the continuous mapping was 0.00294, within palette/engine precision; adornment/body colors matched exactly. The final user-paused sample averaged 0.718 ms per color check, with zero redundant color writes across 64 updates. This is an idle sample, not a moving-frame performance benchmark. Playback rate stayed at 6 ticks/s, UI errors stayed at zero, and installation left camera and all part poses unchanged. Evidence is in evidence/spring-node-colors-20261005.json.
